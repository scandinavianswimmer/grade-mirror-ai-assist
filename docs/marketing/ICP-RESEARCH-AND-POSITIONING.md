# aiTA — ICP Research & Positioning

> Customer research + competitive positioning for aiTA. Mode 2 (gathered intel — no first-party
> transcripts yet). Centered on the **committed ICP: the individual high-school English/humanities
> teacher** (the "Sarah Martinez" archetype). District/higher-ed treated as adjacent expansion.

## 1. The ICP (refined persona)

### Sarah — High-School English / Humanities Teacher

**Profile**
- Teaches writing-heavy subjects (English I–II, AP Lang/Lit, history with essays). 4–6 classes, 120–180 students.
- Public or independent high school; 3–20 years in. High integrity, cares deeply about feedback quality.
- Buys for herself (bottom-up); may later champion to a department/school. Budget sensitivity: high — pays out of pocket or via a small classroom stipend.

**Primary Job to Be Done**
Give every student timely, personalized, rubric-aligned written feedback **without sacrificing nights, weekends, and her own well-being** — and without her feedback degrading into generic comments she's ashamed of.

**Jobs, by layer**
- **Functional:** turn a stack of 150 essays into graded, annotated, commented work fast.
- **Emotional:** stop feeling guilty/exhausted; feel like a *good* teacher again, not a feedback machine. Relief — "the bag of bricks lifted."
- **Social:** feedback that looks like *she* wrote it, so students/parents/admin never sense a machine; protect her professional reputation and student trust.

**Trigger events** (when she goes looking)
- A pile-up: essay unit + report-card deadline collide → grading bleeds into the weekend.
- Burnout spike / considering leaving teaching.
- A colleague or PD session mentions an AI grading tool; she's curious but wary.
- She tried ChatGPT for grading once, it produced robotic/over-positive slop, and she bailed.

**Top pains (in their words — see §3)**
1. Grading eats her life; essay feedback is unpaid overtime.
2. AI tools she's tried feel generic / robotic / "not how I grade."
3. She doesn't trust AI scores — they misapply the rubric, reward the wrong things.
4. She refuses to hand students machine feedback that strips the human relationship.

**Objections & fears**
- "It won't sound like me / students will know." (voice)
- "It'll grade inaccurately and I'll have to redo everything." (trust → net-zero time)
- "Am I cheating my students out of real feedback?" (ethics/identity)
- "Is this how I get replaced?" (existential)
- "Setup will be a fiddly chore." (friction — validated re: CoGrader)
- "Where does my students' data go?" (FERPA/privacy)

**Where to reach her**
- r/Teachers, teacher Facebook groups, ed-Twitter/Bluesky, TPT (Teachers Pay Teachers) audience, school PD, word-of-mouth in the department.
- Content she trusts: other teachers' candid experiences > vendor claims. Demos that show real student work, not marketing reels.

## 2. Top themes (ranked by frequency × intensity)

### Theme 1 — Grading is unsustainable, unpaid cognitive labor *(Confidence: HIGH)*
Essay grading consumes nights and weekends; it's the single most-cited driver of grading fatigue and a contributor to burnout/attrition. Teachers who adopt AI weekly report saving ~5.9 hrs/week (Walton Family Foundation).
**Implication:** time-saving is the *entry ticket*, not the differentiator — every competitor already claims it.

### Theme 2 — Teachers distrust AI scoring accuracy & rubric fidelity *(Confidence: HIGH)*
Recurring, specific complaints: the AI **"took points off for things not in the rubric"**, applied **"standards outside the students' current skill level,"** and grades everything the same way (AP Lit like 5th-grade persuasive) → generic, miscalibrated output. Teachers emphasize the need for human oversight and don't trust automated scores.
**Implication:** *trustworthy, rubric-faithful, level-calibrated grading is the real battleground.* This is aiTA's strongest pillar (deterministic rubric gate + relevance gate + level calibration).

### Theme 3 — "It doesn't sound like me" is the unmet desire *(Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH)*
Generic/robotic AI feedback is the top reason teachers abandon these tools ("teachers spend more time correcting the AI than benefiting from it"). The demand is validated by the market: **CoGrader already markets "still sounds like you"** — but as a prompt claim, not a learning loop.
**Implication:** whoever genuinely *learns and reproduces the teacher's voice* — and proves it — owns the category. This is aiTA's defensible wedge.

### Theme 4 — Human feedback carries weight AI can't replicate; "augment, don't replace" *(Confidence: HIGH)*
Strong consensus (practitioner + academic): human feedback brings contextual awareness of a student's journey and emotional weight; AI should be a "supportive technology, not a replacement." The crisp industry phrase: **"outsource the doing, not the thinking."**
**Implication:** mandatory human-in-the-loop isn't a constraint to apologize for — it's the *positioning*. Lead with it.

### Theme 5 — AI-detection is poisoning teacher–student trust *(Confidence: HIGH)*
AI "cheating" detectors (Turnitin, GPTZero, bundled into EssayGrader) produce damaging false positives (WaPo measured ~50% FP in one study; Vanderbilt *disabled* Turnitin's detector; multiple wrongful-accusation cases, disproportionately hitting ESL/neurodivergent students). Detection creates an atmosphere of default suspicion.
**Implication:** aiTA can **counter-position hard**: pro-teacher *and* pro-student. "We amplify you; we don't police your students." Differentiates from the detection-first incumbents on values, not just features.

### Theme 6 — Setup/parameter friction *(Confidence: MEDIUM)*
Even well-liked tools draw "too complicated and time-consuming to enter the parameters" (CoGrader). Rubric entry is itself a chore.
**Implication:** low-friction onboarding + rubric synthesis-from-prompt is a concrete wedge against the "it's a hassle" objection.

## 4. Competitive positioning map

| Tool | Core promise | Strength | Where it's weak / exposed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EssayGrader** (category leader, 100k+ teachers) | Speed + standards alignment; "hours → minutes" | 500+ rubric library, all-50-states/AP/IB alignment, LMS, big trust numbers | Bundles **AI detection** (trust liability); rubric-applied but **no teacher-voice learning**; "more rubrics" ≠ "your voice" |
| **CoGrader** (best free) | "Less time grading, more time teaching" | Free tier (100/mo, ~$19 paid), simple, "teacher has final say," claims **"sounds like you"** | "Sounds like you" is a **prompt claim, not a learning loop**; users hit **setup/parameter friction**; lighter (no analytics/standards library) |
| **GPTZero Grader** | AI-detection-first grading | Has a **"calibration"** feature (learns grading style) | Detection-centric → adversarial framing; weaker rubric feedback |
| **Turnitin** | Integrity/plagiarism platform | Incumbent, institutional | **Detection false-positive controversy**; expensive; institution-only; surveillance brand |
| **Brisk** | Formative feedback browser extension | In-doc Google workflow | **No rubric scoring / summative grading** — different lane |
| **Gradescope** | Complex/STEM/handwritten, university | Bulk, regrade requests | Not humanities-essay feedback; institutional |
| **MagicSchool** | 80+ teacher tools (breadth) | All-in-one suite | Grading is shallow; jack-of-all-trades |
| **Marking.ai / Writable** | Analytics / district workflows | Dashboards; district integration | No free/individual access (Writable); price (Marking.ai) |

### The white space (where nobody is standing)
1. **A grader that genuinely LEARNS the teacher's voice via a closed edit→reinforce loop** (persistent pedagogical memory) — and *proves* it. Everyone else: static prompts or "more rubrics."
2. **Trust-through-refusal**: an AI that **withholds** a grade on off-topic/garbage work and **stays inside the rubric** — visibly. Directly answers the #1 distrust theme; nobody demonstrates it.
3. **Anti-surveillance values**: pro-student, no punitive AI-detection. A values wedge against the detection-first incumbents.

## 5. Positioning recommendations — offer, app, UI

### 5.1 The core wedge: sell **FIDELITY, not speed**
Speed is table-stakes and a race to the bottom — and it *triggers the replacement fear*. aiTA's headline should be the thing only it can credibly say:

> **"It grades like *you* would — to your rubric, in your voice — and gets more like you every week. You stay the teacher."**

Speed becomes the *byproduct* ("and yes, it gives you your weekend back"), not the lead. This reframes aiTA out of the crowded "fast auto-grader" bucket and into a category of one: *captured teaching expertise.*

**Three supporting territories (use together):**
- **A — "In your voice, and it proves it."** Lead with the voice-learning loop + a visible with/without-style difference. Moat = persistent pedagogical memory.
- **B — "It won't rubber-stamp garbage."** Trust-through-refusal: the off-topic withholding + rubric/relevance gates. Answers the inaccuracy fear head-on. *"A grade you can trust because aiTA knows when* not *to give one."*
- **C — "On the teacher's side — not policing your students."** Counter-position vs. detection-first incumbents. Emotional + values differentiation; removes the adversarial student dynamic.

### 5.2 Offer / packaging
- **Bottom-up freemium** (mirror EssayGrader/CoGrader's proven motion): generous free tier to get the teacher hooked on "it sounds like me." Reframe the free tier as **"Start teaching aiTA your voice."**
- **Switching-cost moat as a feature, not a secret:** every edit the teacher makes trains *their* aiTA. The captured style doesn't transfer to a competitor. Make the accumulating personalization legible ("aiTA has learned 47 of your edits") — this is both a retention hook and a status reward.
- **Honest, FERPA-aware trust framing** in the offer (never "fully compliant" — say exactly what's done: de-identification before the model, owner-scoped data, right-to-erasure). Privacy as a *selling point* given Theme 5 + student-data anxiety.
- Defer hard B2B/Stripe enterprise messaging until the individual-teacher loop is loved; expand to department/school via the in-product champion path.

### 5.3 App (product narrative)
- **"The AI workforce you supervise."** Reframe the (already-built) named multi-agent pipeline — Rubric, Relevance/Risk, Grading, Annotation, Feedback, Style — as a *team the teacher manages*, not a black-box API call. Turns the architecture into a trust story and an X-Prize-grade "agentic" narrative.
- **Make the withholding a hero feature, not an edge case.** Surface "I withheld a grade because this is off-assignment — here's why" prominently. It's the single most disarming trust proof; show it in the demo and onboarding.
- **"Watch it learn you."** The edit-rate-trending-down metric is the *falsifiable* proof of the core promise — make it a hero metric, not buried analytics. "Your edit rate dropped 40% over 3 batches — aiTA is converging on how you grade."

### 5.4 UI (the anti-slop signal)
- **Lean into the "Marginalia" design system** (parchment/pine/ochre, Fraunces + Hanken, annotation-pen affordances). In a sea of sterile blue SaaS dashboards, a UI that feels like *a craftsman's grading desk* signals "this respects the craft of teaching" before a word is read. The aesthetic itself is counter-positioning against "AI slop."
- **Grammarly-style inline annotations in the teacher's voice**, with a one-tap Accept / Edit / Dismiss loop — keep friction near zero (directly counters Theme 6).
- **Show the voice, don't just claim it:** a side-by-side "generic draft vs *your* voice" toggle on a graded essay lets the teacher *see* the personalization. Seeing beats telling for the skeptical ICP.
- **Confidence + evidence citations per criterion** (already built) framed as "aiTA shows its work" — reinforces oversight/trust, not automation.

### 5.5 Messaging do / don't (from the research)
- **DO** lead with voice/trust/relief; name the human-in-the-loop as a virtue; show real student work; quantify learning (edit-rate).
- **DON'T** lead with raw speed; never imply replacement; never bundle or imply punitive AI-detection; avoid "fully compliant" privacy claims; avoid sterile corporate-AI aesthetics.
