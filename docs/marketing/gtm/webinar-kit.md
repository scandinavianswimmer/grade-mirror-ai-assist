# Free-PD Webinar Kit — the #1 ban-proof Facebook motion

> Facebook teacher groups are the top channel but ban direct self-promo (`XPRIZE-MASTER-PLAN.md`). The
> ban-proof way in is to give the group genuine value: a **free 30-minute PD session** that an admin is
> happy to host because it serves their members. aiTA appears only as a brief, honest "here's the tool I
> used" — the value stands without it. Voice from `MESSAGING.md` (teacher-facing: time + voice, never bias).
> Pitch to admins via `outreach/templates/fb-webinar-pitch.md`.

## Positioning
- **Title:** *"Grade a Full Essay Set Faster — While Staying the Final Grader"*
- **Subtitle:** Practical AI workflows for ELA feedback that still sounds like you (no robotic comments).
- **Promise:** leave with a repeatable workflow + a rubric/feedback template you can use Monday.
- **Why admins say yes:** it's PD, not a pitch. You teach the *category* (AI for feedback done right);
  aiTA is one tool you mention once, with full "your mileage may vary" honesty.

## Run-of-show (30 min + 10 Q&A)
| Time | Segment | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–3:00 | **The grading-time problem** (relatable cold open: the weekend essay pile) | Earn trust; name the pain |
| 3:00–8:00 | **Why most teachers quit AI graders** (robotic, miscalibrated, "I redo it") | Validate skepticism; you're one of them |
| 8:00–16:00 | **The 4-part workflow** (rubric-first → draft → *edit in your voice* → approve) | The actual teachable value |
| 16:00–22:00 | **Live demo** on a real essay: voice toggle, off-topic refusal, approve/edit loop | Show, don't tell |
| 22:00–27:00 | **Keeping the teacher in command** (HITL, trust, privacy, no detection) | Address the fears |
| 27:00–30:00 | **Your turn this week** + the free template + (soft) where to try it | One clear next step |
| 30:00–40:00 | **Q&A** | Relationship-building; capture questions for content |

## Deck outline (slide-by-slide)
1. Title + your name + "I teach/build for ELA teachers."
2. The weekend essay pile (one honest image). "Sound familiar?"
3. The tradeoff: speed vs. feedback quality → fatigue → generic comments you're not proud of.
4. Why teachers abandon AI graders: robotic · misapplies the rubric · "I spend more time fixing it."
5. The reframe: **outsource the typing, not the thinking.** You stay the author.
6. Workflow step 1 — **Rubric first** (paste your prompt; let AI draft the rubric; you edit).
7. Workflow step 2 — **Draft** rubric-aligned scores + inline comments.
8. Workflow step 3 — **Edit in your voice** (the part that matters — every edit teaches the tool you).
9. Workflow step 4 — **Approve.** Nothing reaches a student you didn't sign off on.
10. **Live demo** placeholder: voice toggle (generic vs. yours) · the off-topic refusal · approve/edit.
11. Trust: it **withholds** off-topic grades; shows evidence per criterion; you can override anything.
12. Privacy done honestly: names stripped before the model; your data is yours; no AI-detection.
13. **Your turn this week:** try the 4-step workflow on one set. Free template link.
14. (Soft) "The tool I used is aiTA — free 14-day trial on sample essays, no student data. Try it or don't;
    the workflow works with any tool that lets you stay the editor." + your contact.
15. Q&A / thank you / where to follow you.
