## Slide 3 — The real tradeoff
**On-screen:**
> ### The tradeoff that burns us out
> **Speed ⟷ Quality.** Grade fast → generic comments you're not proud of. Grade well → your weekend's gone.

**Talk track:** "The trap isn't that grading is slow. It's the tradeoff: when we speed up, the feedback gets
generic, and we know it. When we keep the quality, we pay with our evenings. Most 'solutions' just ask you

## Slide 4 — Why AI grading didn't stick
**On-screen:**
> ### You've tried AI. It didn't stick. Here's why:
> - It misapplies your rubric — points off for things you never asked for
> - It grades a 9th-grader like a college senior
> - It sounds like a bot — so you rewrite every comment

**Talk track:** "Be honest — most of you have tried ChatGPT or an AI grader and bailed. Not because you're
anti-AI. Because it took points off for things not in your rubric, miscalibrated the level, and the
comments sounded nothing like you. The line I hear most: 'I spent more time fixing the AI than I'd have
spent grading.' That's a real failure. The fix isn't more automation."

---

## Slide 5 — The reframe
**On-screen:**
> ### Outsource the typing. Not the thinking.
> You stay the author. AI drafts; you decide.

**Talk track:** "Here's the reframe that makes AI actually useful: you outsource the *typing*, not the
*thinking*. You stay the author of the feedback. The AI's job is to draft — yours is to judge, edit, and
approve. Keep that mental model and everything that follows works with basically any tool that lets you
stay the editor."

---

## Slide 6 — Workflow step 1: Rubric first
**On-screen:**
> ### Step 1 — Rubric first
> No rubric handy? Paste your prompt → let AI draft the rubric → you edit it.
> *The rubric is the guardrail. Set it before any grading.*

**Talk track:** "Step one, and it's the most important: rubric first. The single biggest reason AI grades
badly is that it's grading without your rubric, so it invents its own standards. If you have a rubric,
load it. If you don't, paste the assignment prompt and have the AI draft one — then *you* edit it. Two
minutes here saves the whole set. The rubric is the guardrail."

---

## Slide 7 — Workflow step 2: Draft
**On-screen:**
> ### Step 2 — Draft
> AI produces rubric-aligned scores + inline comments + a summary.
> *A starting point — not a verdict.*

**Talk track:** "Step two: let it draft. Rubric-aligned scores, inline margin comments, a summary. Treat it
as a first draft of the feedback, not a verdict. The good tools show their evidence per criterion — 'I gave
this a 3 because of these two sentences' — so you can check the reasoning, not just the number."

---

## Slide 8 — Workflow step 3: Edit in your voice
**On-screen:**
> ### Step 3 — Edit in YOUR voice  ← the part that matters
> Tweak a few comments to sound like you. Good tools *learn* from those edits.

**Talk track:** "Step three is where the magic is. You go through and tweak the comments to sound like you —
your phrasing, your warmth, your standards. With a static tool you do this every single time. With a tool
that *learns*, every edit teaches it your voice, so next set it needs fewer corrections. This is the
difference between an AI that stays generic and one that converges on *you*."

---

## Slide 9 — Workflow step 4: Approve
**On-screen:**
> ### Step 4 — Approve
> Nothing reaches a student you didn't sign off on. You're the final grader — always.

**Talk track:** "Step four: approve. This is the guardrail that lets you trust the whole thing. Nothing goes
to a student you didn't see and sign off on. You are the final grader, by design — not a rubber stamp on a
machine's decision. That's the line we never cross."

## Slide 11 — Trust mechanics
**On-screen:**
> ### Why you can trust it
> - **Refuses** off-topic / off-assignment work (withholds the grade)
> - **Evidence + confidence** per criterion — it shows its work
> - **You can override** anything, anytime

**Talk track:** "Let's name the trust mechanics, because skepticism here is healthy. It withholds grades on
off-topic work instead of bluffing. It shows evidence and a confidence level per criterion. And you can
override any score or comment. Trust isn't 'the AI is always right' — it's 'the AI tells you when it isn't,
and you're in control.'"

**On-screen:**
> ### Privacy — said plainly
> - Student names **stripped before** anything is processed
> - Your data is **yours** — and erasable
> - **No** AI-detection. We don't police your students.

*and* your students'."

## Q&A prep (anticipated — answers from `MESSAGING.md`)
- **"Does it really sound like me?"** → It learns from your edits and you can see the side-by-side; it's
  measured, not just claimed.
- **"What about accuracy/hallucination?"** → Rubric-grounded, evidence per criterion, refuses off-topic,
  you approve everything.
- **"Is this replacing teachers?"** → Opposite — you're the final grader; it handles the typing, you keep
  the judgment.
- **"FERPA / data?"** → De-identified before the model, owner-scoped, erasable; the trial uses sample
  essays. We never say "fully compliant."
- **"Cost?"** → $15/mo or ~$150/yr, 14-day full trial, free 15-grade tier.
- **"Which tools do this?"** → Name the workflow first; mention aiTA as the one that learns voice + refuses.
