## Project description — target 750–900 words

On Sunday night, the hard part of an essay stack is not writing one thoughtful comment. It is doing
that again and again without drifting from the assignment, overlooking a student’s evidence, or
flattening your own standards into generic advice. Teachers do not need another blank chat box. They
need a careful first pass they can inspect, correct, and make their own.

Mr Selby is a teacher-controlled grading co-pilot. The name is a personal tribute to a teacher whose
assignments, feedback, and standards made careful teaching feel visible; it does not imply his
affiliation or endorsement. The product starts from the materials that actually govern the work: the
assignment, rubric, student response, and teacher-approved style context. It extracts the submission,
checks whether it belongs to the assignment, drafts criterion-level feedback, locates support in the
paper, and recomputes totals on the server. The result is a review workspace, not an automatic verdict.

We designed the judge journey around four questions we call the Teacher’s Test:

1. Does the draft follow the assignment and rubric?
2. Can it point to evidence in the paper?
3. Does it stop when the work should not be graded?
4. Can the teacher revise every margin note before approval?

The public Judge Mode answers those questions with an original fictional fixture, “The Beacon
Ledger.” One response offers a strong, on-topic analysis. A second is deliberately unrelated to the
assignment. The first lets a judge inspect exact passages and try the accept, edit, and dismiss
controls. The second produces a safe “Needs a closer look” state with no proposed score. The page
labels the assignment, learners, draft note, and outcomes as fictional. It never presents that fixture
as a customer record, model run, production trace, or measured result.
